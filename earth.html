How do we fund this on Earth without companies fleeing offshore? We tax economic flows and assets that cannot leave. First, a Destination-Based Cash-Flow Tax (DBCFT / border-adjusted VAT) paired with 100% immediate expensing for all capital investments. Second, a Georgist Land Value Tax (LVT) on unimproved urban locations, alongside royalties on raw energy throughput and mineral extraction.

To see how the arithmetic balances in a terrestrial economy, look at the concrete balance sheet of the United States:

  • Macroeconomic Scale (US Benchmark): US GDP stands at approximately $28 to $30 trillion, with total private consumption expenditures exceeding $19 trillion and commercial cash flows approaching $4 trillion. Privately held US real estate exceeds $45 trillion, of which raw unimproved land value accounts for roughly $18 to $20 trillion.
  • The Revenue Engine: A clean 15% destination-based consumption levy on domestic retail transactions yields roughly $2.85 trillion. Pairing this with a 5% Land Value Tax on unimproved location rents collects an additional $1.0 trillion, while energy royalties (kWh grid throughput) and resource extractions contribute $600 billion.
  • The Net Distribution Floor: This closed-flow engine generates $4.45 trillion annually in stable, non-evadable revenue. Distributed across roughly 260 million adult citizens, this funds an unconditional baseline dividend of $17,100 per adult per year (or over $34,000 for a two-adult household)—completely eliminating biological poverty without taking on sovereign debt or taxing capital formation.

Because CapEx receives 100% immediate expensing, businesses pay zero tax on what they reinvest into robotics, factories, or energy grids; taxes fall purely on net distributions and extracted land rents at consumption.
